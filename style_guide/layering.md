# Layering Guidance

## Structured Layers

Structured layers such as blazers and tailored jackets can increase an outfit's polish by adding structure, coverage, and visual intentionality.

A relatively casual base top combined with tailored bottoms and a structured blazer may become suitable for smart-casual or some business-casual contexts.

The effect depends on the complete combination. A structured layer should not be treated as an automatic formality upgrade.

## Soft Layers

Cardigans and other soft knit layers add coverage and can make an outfit appear more intentional without providing the same degree of structure as a tailored jacket.

They work especially well in casual, smart-casual, and many business-casual contexts.

For conservative or high-stakes professional occasions, a structured layer may rank above a soft layer when both combinations are otherwise appropriate.

## Coverage Through Layering

Sleeveless, sheer, low-cut, cropped, or otherwise revealing garments may become suitable for additional contexts when combined with opaque base garments or sufficient outer coverage.

Coverage should be evaluated from the final visible outfit rather than from individual pieces in isolation.

The completed layering arrangement must still satisfy the expectations of the occasion.

## Layering Does Not Fix Everything

Adding a blazer, cardigan, or other polished outer layer does not automatically make an unsuitable outfit appropriate.

Highly casual graphics, excessive exposure, distressed garments, incompatible silhouettes, or other strongly informal elements may remain dominant after layering.

A layer should improve the complete composition rather than function as a universal override.

## Functional Layering

Layers should contribute meaningfully to the outfit through structure, coverage, warmth, color balance, visual interest, or occasion suitability.

Additional layers are not automatically better. Once an outfit appropriately satisfies the occasion, unnecessary layering can make the combination overdressed, visually heavy, or impractical.
